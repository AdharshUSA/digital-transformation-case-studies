# Digital Transformation Case Studies: Bosch, C3 and ERP Technology

Three team presentations for **IE 551: Digital Transformation in Industrial Businesses**, Purdue University (Spring 2026). Team of three.

## 1. Bosch: from products to platforms

An analysis of how Bosch has pursued digital transformation since 2017, moving from selling hardware to running IoT and software platforms.

- **Value creation:** IoT suite and SaaS-style predictive maintenance, AI built into products, a platform and marketplace strategy, data monetization, and outcome-as-a-service business models.
- **Execution:** connected plants and digital twins, a large software and AI hiring push, hybrid cloud with a unified data lake, and an agile and cultural change program.
- **Risks:** cybersecurity at the scale of millions of connected devices, legacy systems, talent and culture, competition from software-native rivals, geopolitical and regulatory pressure, and uncertain ROI.
- **Outlook to 2030:** the 2026 to 2030 window is when software revenue has to prove itself before hardware margins erode.

## 2. C3: enabling digital industrial transformation

The story of how C3 changed its focus four times: carbon measurement (2009), the C3 Energy smart grid platform (2012), C3 IoT (2016) and finally C3 AI.

- **Why it pivoted:** each shift followed where the platform's real value was, from carbon analytics to grid sensor data to a general-purpose AI and IoT platform.
- **Customer approach:** paid 8 to 12 week trials, intensive client training, and a move toward self-service tooling.
- **Culture:** a flat hierarchy and a deliberate preference for generalists over domain specialists.
- **Challenges:** scaling headcount, the talent war for data scientists, moving customers to self-sufficiency, and the learning curve of the C3 Type System.
- **Takeaway:** a model-driven architecture that tackles fragmented enterprise data is what let C3 move across industries.

## 3. ERP technology

A technology overview of enterprise resource planning, from history to future trends.

- **Evolution:** MRP, MRP II, ERP, ERP II, cloud ERP, and today's AI-enabled ERP.
- **Core modules and architecture:** finance, supply chain, HR, manufacturing, inventory, CRM and procurement, with on-premise, cloud and hybrid deployment models.
- **Trends:** AI and machine learning, intelligent automation, IoT, blockchain, digital twins and generative AI copilots.
- **Implementation:** the eight-step lifecycle from requirements to post-go-live support, and the common failure points such as cost, change management and data migration.
- **Vendors and integration:** SAP S/4HANA, Oracle, Microsoft Dynamics 365, NetSuite, Odoo and ERPNext, plus REST APIs, iPaaS middleware, EDI and event-driven integration.
- **Case studies and future:** Toyota, Walmart, Cleveland Clinic and the US Department of Defense, then composable, autonomous and industry-cloud ERP.

## Note

These are research and analysis presentations based on public information about each company and technology. They are not original data studies.
